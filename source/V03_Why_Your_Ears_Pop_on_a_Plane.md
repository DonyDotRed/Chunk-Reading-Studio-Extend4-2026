## 0. 원문 (Full Text)

> If you have ever flown in an airplane, you have probably felt a strange fullness in your ears as the plane climbs or descends, followed by a small "pop." This everyday experience is a lesson in air pressure.
>
> Your middle ear is a small, air-filled space behind the eardrum. Normally, the air pressure inside it is the same as the pressure outside your body. When the two pressures are equal, the eardrum can vibrate freely, and you hear clearly.
>
> Air pressure, however, changes with altitude. Although airplane cabins are pressurized, the pressure inside a cruising aircraft is still lower than at sea level, roughly equal to the pressure on a mountain about two thousand meters high. As the plane climbs, cabin pressure falls. The air trapped in your middle ear is now at a higher pressure than the air outside, so it pushes the eardrum outward. As the plane descends, the opposite happens, and the eardrum is pushed inward. In both cases, the stretched eardrum vibrates less easily, which is why sounds may seem muffled.
>
> The solution is a narrow passage called the Eustachian tube, which connects the middle ear to the back of the nose and throat. Most of the time, this tube is closed. When you swallow or yawn, muscles briefly open it, allowing air to flow in or out until the pressures are equal again. The "pop" you hear is the sound of the eardrum returning to its normal position.
>
> Descent is usually more uncomfortable than climbing. Air leaves the middle ear relatively easily, but getting air back in requires the tube to open actively. This is why chewing gum, swallowing often, or yawning during landing can help. If you have a cold, the tube may be swollen and blocked, making equalization difficult and sometimes painful. For that reason, doctors sometimes advise against flying with a severe cold.
>
> Babies often cry during landing for the same reason. They cannot yet equalize pressure on purpose, but sucking on a bottle or pacifier helps them swallow, which opens the tube.
>
> So the next time your ears pop, you can think of it as your body's own pressure valve at work.

### 3. 과학적 근거에 대한 주석

순항 중 객실 기압은 대략 해발 1,800–2,400미터(6,000–8,000피트)에 해당하도록 유지되는 경우가 많아, 본문에서는 "약 2,000미터"로 표현했습니다. 상승 시보다 하강 시 귀가 더 불편한 이유(공기가 중이에서 나가는 것은 비교적 수동적으로 일어나지만 들어가려면 이관이 열려야 함)는 항공의학에서 널리 설명되는 기전입니다. 이 글은 교양 해설이며, 귀 통증이 심하거나 오래가면 의료진과 상담하는 것이 좋습니다.
